Background

The biology behind the tasks

Each task has one right answer that is fixed by the experiment or by expert annotation, and each carries a trap built from how paced cardiac tissue and immature cardiomyocytes behave. I ran each as an autonomous agent task on Anthropic and OpenAI models, three runs each, graded with SpatialBench grader types.

Engineered heart tissues

Heart muscle cells made from human stem cells are grown into small strips stretched between two flexible pillars. The strips beat on their own, and labs use them to study disease variants and test drugs. Each contraction bends the pillars, and a brightfield camera records it.

Pacing and capture

Labs often pace these tissues with an electrical field, for example once a second (1 Hz), so every tissue beats at the same rate. A tissue is captured when every stimulus produces exactly one beat, which locks its beat interval to the stimulus period. A tissue whose own rate is above the pacing rate keeps its own rhythm, and its beats drift ahead of the stimulus.

Sarcomeres

The sarcomere is the repeating contractile unit of heart muscle. A fluorescent tag on alpha-actinin marks its z-lines, so a mature cell shows regular stripes, about 1.7 µm apart in these cells. Immature cells show scattered dots and fibers instead, and have no sarcomere length to measure.

Why this tests an AI agent

Benchmarks like SpatialBench measure whether agents can analyze real biological data on their own. Reading a frame rate or an image axis correctly tests care. Knowing what capture means, or when there is no sarcomere to measure, tests whether the agent understands the biology it is analyzing.

    B2ACTN2-mEGFP confocal · 8 cells

    Which cells have sarcomeres, and how long are they?

    Eight day-32 hiPSC-cardiomyocytes from one imaging session, each with its mask. The agent decides which cells have striated myofibrils with measurable z-lines, then reports the median sarcomere length across only those cells. Striation calls come from the dataset's two expert scorers (4 to 5 versus 1 to 2 on their 5-point scale, no borderline cells), and agree with the authors' classifier.

    Design

    How I build a trap

    The experiment sets the answer

    A tissue paced at 1 Hz has a known period, and the dataset's expert scorers decide which cells are striated. The analysis measures each tissue or cell against that fixed reference.

    The trap comes from the biology

    A frame-rate slip tests care. Recognizing that a tissue beating 3% faster than its stimulus is not captured by it tests training in cardiac physiology.

    The prompt gives what an analyst would have

    It quotes the depositors' 30 Hz frame rate because that is what anyone opening the deposit would read, and it never names the trap.

    I raise the bar until the strongest agent is tested

    I drop a design once its answer stops being unambiguous. A twitch-force task and a paced-versus-spontaneous task were dropped for that reason.

    Development

    How the tasks got harder

    The first versions trapped signal processing: a frame rate, a harmonic, an image axis. Every Claude run cleared them. GPT-6 Luna passed every sarcomere run but missed the beat rate in all three runs, answering 55 to 56 bpm for a tissue paced at 60. The second versions move the trap into the biology, what capture of a pacing stimulus means and when there is no sarcomere to measure. On capture, two of the three misses moved from the time base to the biology, counting tissues near 60 bpm as captured. A3 then took away the shortcut of regular beating close to 1 Hz, and only Opus passed every run.

    A1 · beat rate of one paced tissue

    Answer 60 bpm, fixed by the 1 Hz pacing. A motion trace has a contraction and a relaxation peak per beat (120 bpm if both are counted), the third harmonic tops the spectrum (180 bpm), and the sheet's 30 Hz frame rate gives 54.5 to 54.7 bpm against the file's own 32.9 fps timestamps.

    B1 · sarcomere length of one cell

    Answer 1.72 µm by two independent methods, within one pixel of the authors' value for the cell. Its myofibrils run at about 44°, so measurements along the image axes give 1.91 to 2.39 µm, and an answer left in pixels gives 14.1.

    Method

    How the runs were made

    1. Format. Each eval follows the SpatialBench format: a fixed answer block, and notes giving the ground truth, the traps, a separation table and the tolerance rationale. Graded with numeric_tolerance, label_set_jaccard and all_of, checked against the latch-eval-tools grader classes.
    2. Ground truth. Every number in the trap tables comes from ground_truth.py, run on source files that fetch_data.py downloads and verifies by checksum.
    3. Agents. Each model ran in a fresh directory with only the data and shell access to Python: Claude through Claude Code, isolated from any personal configuration, and GPT through the Codex CLI in its workspace sandbox.
    4. Input. A short fixed environment note plus the task prompt was the only input, identical across models. The prompts state what an analyst would know, including the depositors' 30 Hz figure, and never name a trap.
