Benchmark tasks for agentic microscopy analysis

Beats and Z-Lines

Two benchmark tasks built from public cardiac microscopy data. Each has one right answer that is fixed by the experiment or by expert annotation, and each carries a trap built from how paced cardiac tissue and immature cardiomyocytes behave. I tested both as autonomous agents on Anthropic and OpenAI models, three runs each, graded with SpatialBench grader types. Every Claude run passed. GPT-6 Luna failed three of its six runs, each time on the planted trap: it counted tissues beating at 57 and 62 bpm as captured by a 1 Hz stimulus, and it called immature cells striated. Google's Gemini completed one run of a first-version task before its free-tier daily quota ran out.

Task A2 · brightfield time-lapse

Which paced tissues are captured?

Eleven engineered hiPSC-cardiomyocyte microbundles were recorded on day 7 under 1 Hz field stimulation. The agent decides which tissues follow the stimulus 1:1. The pacing frequency is the experiment's own ground truth: a captured tissue beats exactly at the stimulus period.

Answer: 8 captured; not captured Ex00636, Ex00637, Ex00638

Every interval in the captured tissues sits within 0.010 s of 1.000 s. Each non-captured tissue has an interval at least 0.079 s off and drifts at least 0.137 s against the pacing grid. Ex00637 has intervals as short as 0.89 s, which a 1 Hz stimulus cannot produce.

Task B2 · ACTN2-mEGFP confocal

Which cells have sarcomeres, and how long are they?

Eight day-32 hiPSC-cardiomyocytes from one imaging session, each with its mask. The agent decides which cells have striated myofibrils with measurable z-lines, then reports the median sarcomere length across only those cells. Striation calls come from the dataset's two expert scorers (4 to 5 versus 1 to 2 on their 5-point scale, no borderline cells), and agree with the authors' classifier.

Answer: cells B, D, E; median 1.73 µm (±0.12)

A Fourier analysis or a line profile returns a number for every cell, including the immature ones whose alpha-actinin is punctate or fibrillar.

How the tasks got harder

The first versions trapped signal processing: a frame rate, a harmonic, an image axis. Every Claude run cleared them, GPT passed 3 of 6, and Gemini's completed run took the sheet's 30 Hz at face value. The second versions move the trap into the biology, what capture of a pacing stimulus means and when there is no sarcomere to measure, so passing them takes domain knowledge rather than careful arithmetic. Claude again cleared every run, and GPT's failures moved from the frame rate to the biology.

A1 · beat rate of one paced tissue

Answer 60 bpm, fixed by the 1 Hz pacing. A motion trace has a contraction and a relaxation peak per beat (120 bpm if both are counted), the third harmonic tops the spectrum (180 bpm), and the sheet's 30 Hz frame rate gives 54.5 to 54.7 bpm against the file's own 32.9 fps timestamps.

B1 · sarcomere length of one cell

Answer 1.72 µm by two independent methods, within one pixel of the authors' value for the cell. Its myofibrils run at about 44°, so measurements along the image axes give 1.91 to 2.39 µm, and an answer left in pixels gives 14.1.

Method

  • Each eval follows the SpatialBench format: the task prompt ends in a fixed answer block, and the notes give the ground truth, the traps, a separation table of approaches actually run, and the tolerance rationale. Grading uses numeric_tolerance, label_set_jaccard and all_of, checked against the latch-eval-tools grader classes.
  • Every number in the trap tables comes from a reproducible ground_truth.py run on the shipped data.
  • Each model ran as an agent in a fresh directory holding only the data, with shell access to Python: Claude through Claude Code, GPT through the Codex CLI in its workspace sandbox, Gemini through the Gemini CLI in its macOS sandbox. Prompts were identical across models. Full transcripts are kept.
  • The prompts state what an analyst would know, including the depositors' 30 Hz figure, and never name a trap.
