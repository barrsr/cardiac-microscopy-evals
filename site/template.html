Beats and Z-Lines

Benchmark tasks built from public cardiac microscopy data. Each has one right answer that is fixed by the experiment or by expert annotation, and each carries a trap built from how paced cardiac tissue and immature cardiomyocytes behave. I ran each as an autonomous agent task on Anthropic and OpenAI models, three runs each, graded with SpatialBench grader types.

    Tasks

    Three main tasks

    A3Brightfield time-lapse · 19 tissues

    Captured, or escaping the stimulus?

    Nineteen engineered hiPSC-cardiomyocyte microbundles from a patient line carrying an HCM-associated MYBPC3 variant, recorded on day 7 under 1 Hz field stimulation across three imaging sessions. The agent decides which tissues follow the stimulus 1:1. The stimulus is not recorded in the images.

    Answer6 captured (Ex00560, Ex00561, Ex00567, Ex00569, Ex00570, Ex00572); the other 13 are not

    Ex00575 and Ex00576 beat as regularly as several of the captured tissues, at 0.96 to 0.98 s per beat. Each beat arrives before the next stimulus could have triggered it, so these tissues are running their own rhythm a little faster than the pacing. Every captured interval sits within 0.010 s of 1.000 s; the closest escaping tissue, Ex00575, is 0.024 to 0.035 s short on every interval.

    A2Brightfield time-lapse · 11 tissues

    Which paced tissues are captured?

    Eleven engineered hiPSC-cardiomyocyte microbundles were recorded on day 7 under 1 Hz field stimulation. The agent decides which tissues follow the stimulus 1:1. The pacing frequency is the experiment's own ground truth: a captured tissue beats exactly at the stimulus period.

    Answer8 captured; not captured Ex00636, Ex00637, Ex00638

    Every interval in the captured tissues sits within 0.010 s of 1.000 s. Each non-captured tissue has an interval at least 0.079 s off and drifts at least 0.137 s against the pacing grid. Ex00637 has intervals as short as 0.89 s, which a 1 Hz stimulus cannot produce.

    B2ACTN2-mEGFP confocal · 8 cells

    Which cells have sarcomeres, and how long are they?

    Eight day-32 hiPSC-cardiomyocytes from one imaging session, each with its mask. The agent decides which cells have striated myofibrils with measurable z-lines, then reports the median sarcomere length across only those cells. Striation calls come from the dataset's two expert scorers (4 to 5 versus 1 to 2 on their 5-point scale, no borderline cells), and agree with the authors' classifier.

    Answercells B, D, E; median 1.73 µm (±0.12)

    A Fourier analysis or a line profile returns a number for every cell, including the immature ones whose alpha-actinin is punctate or fibrillar. Every model passed every run of this panel: a masked spectrum with a check for a sarcomere peak is enough.

    Design

    How I build a trap

    The experiment sets the answer

    A tissue paced at 1 Hz has a known period, and the dataset's expert scorers decide which cells are striated. The analysis measures each tissue or cell against that fixed reference.

    The trap comes from the biology

    A frame-rate slip tests care. Recognizing that a tissue beating 3% faster than its stimulus has escaped it tests training in cardiac physiology.

    The prompt gives what an analyst would have

    It quotes the depositors' 30 Hz frame rate because that is what anyone opening the deposit would read, and it never names the trap.

    I raise the bar until the strongest agent is tested

    I drop a design once its answer stops being unambiguous. A twitch-force task and a paced-versus-spontaneous task were dropped for that reason.

    Development

    How the tasks got harder

    The first versions trapped signal processing: a frame rate, a harmonic, an image axis. Every Claude run cleared them. GPT-6 Luna passed every sarcomere run but missed the beat rate in all three runs, answering 55 to 56 bpm for a tissue paced at 60. The second versions move the trap into the biology, what capture of a pacing stimulus means and when there is no sarcomere to measure. On capture, the misses moved from the time base to the biology. A3 then took away the shortcut of regular beating close to 1 Hz, and only Opus passed every run.

    A1 · beat rate of one paced tissue

    Answer 60 bpm, fixed by the 1 Hz pacing. A motion trace has a contraction and a relaxation peak per beat (120 bpm if both are counted), the third harmonic tops the spectrum (180 bpm), and the sheet's 30 Hz frame rate gives 54.5 to 54.7 bpm against the file's own 32.9 fps timestamps.

    B1 · sarcomere length of one cell

    Answer 1.72 µm by two independent methods, within one pixel of the authors' value for the cell. Its myofibrils run at about 44°, so measurements along the image axes give 1.91 to 2.39 µm, and an answer left in pixels gives 14.1.

    Method

    How the runs were made

    1. Each eval follows the SpatialBench format: the task prompt ends in a fixed answer block, and the notes give the ground truth, the traps, a separation table of approaches actually run, and the tolerance rationale. Grading uses numeric_tolerance, label_set_jaccard and all_of, checked against the latch-eval-tools grader classes.
    2. Every number in the trap tables comes from a reproducible ground_truth.py run on the source files that fetch_data.py downloads.
    3. Each model ran as an agent in a fresh directory holding only the data, with shell access to Python: Claude through Claude Code, isolated from any personal configuration, and GPT through the Codex CLI in its workspace sandbox. The task prompt was the only input, identical across models. Full transcripts are kept.
    4. The prompts state what an analyst would know, including the depositors' 30 Hz figure, and never name a trap.
