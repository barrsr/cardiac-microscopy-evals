01 · The biology

The biology behind the tasks

Every task has one right answer set by the experiment or by expert annotation. The trap in each one comes from how paced heart tissue or immature heart cells behave. I ran every task as an autonomous agent on Anthropic and OpenAI models, three runs per model, and graded the answers with SpatialBench grader types.

Engineered heart tissues

Heart muscle cells made from human stem cells are grown into small strips stretched between two flexible pillars. The strips beat on their own, and labs use them to study disease variants and test drugs. Each contraction bends the pillars, and a brightfield camera records it.

Pacing and capture

Labs often pace these tissues with an electrical field at a fixed rate such as once per second (1 Hz). A tissue is captured when every stimulus produces exactly one beat, which locks its beat interval to the stimulus period. A tissue whose own rate is above the pacing rate keeps its own rhythm, and its beats drift ahead of the stimulus.

Sarcomeres

The sarcomere is the repeating contractile unit of heart muscle. A fluorescent tag on alpha-actinin marks its z-lines. In a mature cell they form regular stripes about 1.7 µm apart. Immature cells show scattered dots and fibers instead and have no sarcomere length to measure.

Why this tests an AI agent

Benchmarks like SpatialBench measure whether agents can analyze real biological data on their own. Most analysis mistakes can be caught with careful checking. These tasks are built so that the right answer also depends on knowing what capture means and when a cell has no sarcomeres to measure.

Each task starts from a question that a cardiac biologist would answer quickly.

02 · The test

Captured or beating on its own?

Nineteen tissues were paced once a second and filmed. Each agent received the recordings and had to say which tissues were following the stimulus.

A3Brightfield time-lapse · 19 tissues

Nineteen engineered hiPSC-cardiomyocyte microbundles from a patient line carrying an HCM-associated MYBPC3 variant, recorded on day 7 under 1 Hz field stimulation across three imaging sessions. The agent decides which tissues follow the stimulus 1:1. The stimulus is not recorded in the images.

Answer6 captured (Ex00560, Ex00561, Ex00567, Ex00569, Ex00570, Ex00572); the other 13 are not

Ex00575 and Ex00576 beat as regularly as several of the captured tissues, at 0.96 to 0.98 s per beat. Each beat arrives before the next stimulus could have triggered it, so these tissues are running their own rhythm a little faster than the pacing. Every captured interval sits within 0.010 s of 1.000 s; the closest uncaptured tissue, Ex00575, is 0.024 to 0.035 s short on every interval.

    05 · Getting harder

    How the tasks got harder

    In the first versions the traps were in the signal processing, such as a misleading frame rate or measuring along the wrong image axis. Every Claude run passed them. GPT-6 Luna passed every sarcomere run but missed the beat rate in all three runs, answering 55 to 56 bpm for a tissue paced at 60. The second versions put the trap in the biology. On the capture task, two of the three misses counted tissues beating near 60 bpm as captured. A3 removed the remaining shortcut of regular beating close to 1 Hz, and only Opus passed every run.

    A1First version · 1 tissue

    What is this tissue's beat rate?

    The answer is 60 bpm, set by the 1 Hz pacing. A motion trace has a contraction and a relaxation peak per beat (120 bpm if both are counted), the third harmonic tops the spectrum (180 bpm), and the sheet's 30 Hz frame rate gives 54.5 to 54.7 bpm against the file's own 32.9 fps timestamps.

    Eleven engineered hiPSC-cardiomyocyte microbundles were recorded on day 7 under 1 Hz field stimulation. The agent decides which tissues follow the stimulus 1:1. Because a captured tissue beats exactly at the stimulus period, the pacing itself supplies the ground truth.

    Answer8 captured; not captured Ex00636, Ex00637, Ex00638

    06 · Sarcomeres

    Sarcomeres in confocal images

    These tasks use fluorescence images of the sarcomere's z-lines. Immature cells have no sarcomeres, but a Fourier analysis or a line profile still returns a number for them.

    B2ACTN2-mEGFP confocal · 8 cells

    Which cells have sarcomeres, and how long are they?

    Eight day-32 hiPSC-cardiomyocytes from one imaging session, each with its mask. The agent decides which cells have striated myofibrils with measurable z-lines, then reports the median sarcomere length across only those cells. Striation calls come from the dataset's two expert scorers (4 to 5 versus 1 to 2 on their 5-point scale, no borderline cells), and agree with the authors' classifier.

    07 · Design

    How I build a trap

    Ground truth

    A tissue paced at 1 Hz has a known period, and the dataset's expert scorers decide which cells are striated. The analysis measures each tissue or cell against that fixed reference.

    Where the trap sits

    A wrong frame rate can be caught by careful checking. Recognizing that a tissue beating 3% faster than its stimulus is not captured takes training in cardiac physiology.

    What the prompt says

    It quotes the depositors' 30 Hz frame rate because that is what anyone opening the deposit would read, and it never names the trap.

    When to drop a task

    I drop a design once its answer stops being unambiguous. A twitch-force task and a paced-versus-spontaneous task were dropped for that reason.

    08 · Method

    How the runs were made

    1. Format. Each eval follows the SpatialBench format: a fixed answer block, and notes giving the ground truth, the traps, a separation table and the tolerance rationale. Graded with numeric_tolerance, label_set_jaccard and all_of, checked against the latch-eval-tools grader classes.
    2. Ground truth. Every number in the trap tables comes from ground_truth.py, run on source files that fetch_data.py downloads and verifies by checksum.
    3. Agents. Each model ran in a fresh directory with only the data and shell access to Python: Claude through Claude Code, isolated from any personal configuration, and GPT through the Codex CLI in its workspace sandbox.
    4. Input. A short fixed environment note plus the task prompt was the only input, identical across models. The prompts state what an analyst would know, including the depositors' 30 Hz figure, and never name a trap.
